# Homepage v3 — Copy Review

Section-by-section. Every piece of copy that hits the screen, evaluated for length, clarity, tone, and whether it earns the space it takes.

---

## 1. Hero

```
Eyebrow:  Pasadena healing classes
H1:       SPACES THAT ENCOURAGE HEALING TO HAPPEN
Subhead:  Heart Center LA offers classes, breathwork, relational practice,
          and community experiences designed to help you see, name, and
          change the patterns that keep you stuck.
CTAs:     Book a Class  |  Explore Classes
```

**The headline is the motto. It works. Leave it.**

**The subhead is too long.** It's 27 words doing three jobs: naming the brand ("Heart Center LA offers"), listing four product categories ("classes, breathwork, relational practice, and community experiences"), and explaining the method ("designed to help you see, name, and change the patterns"). At 86px uppercase headline text, the hero already carries a lot of visual weight. The subhead should be one clean breath under it, not a paragraph.

Problems:
- "Heart Center LA offers" — the reader is *on* the Heart Center LA website. This is redundant. Cut it.
- "classes, breathwork, relational practice, and community experiences" — four items the reader doesn't have context for yet. This is an inventory, not an invitation. The At a Glance section exists to do this job.
- "designed to help you see, name, and change" — this is teaching ARC vocabulary before the reader is ready. The method section comes later.
- "the patterns that keep you stuck" — ends on the deficit. The headline just said "healing to happen" (hopeful, forward) and now the subhead pulls the reader backward into stuck.

The subhead needs to be one sentence, under 15 words, that tells the visitor *who this is for* or *what happens here* — without listing products or teaching vocabulary.

Something in the register of:
- "Healing classes and community practice in Pasadena."
- "Where you learn to notice what you carry — and put it down."
- "Somatic healing, relational practice, and community. Pasadena, CA."

## 2. Booking Band

```
Intro:    Find your class
Sub:      Choose the level of support you need today.
Tiers:    Refresh $35  |  Reset $84  |  Restore $196  |  Revitalize $238/mo
CTA:      Reserve Refresh
Desc:     For light renewal when you are functional but tired.
```

**"Find your class" — clean. "Choose the level of support you need today" — excellent.** Normalizes needing support, gives agency, uses "today" to lower commitment. No notes on either.

**The tier descriptions (in data attributes) are uneven:**
- Refresh: "For light renewal when you are functional but tired." — Perfect. Specific, honest, no jargon.
- Reset: "For interrupting a pattern and finding a clean slate." — Good. "Clean slate" is concrete.
- Restore: "For rebuilding capacity after depletion, grief, or chronic stress." — Good. Names real conditions.
- Revitalize: "For a full month of classes and deeper renewal." — Weak. This describes duration and intensity but not *what's different*. The other three name a state ("tired," "clean slate," "depletion/grief"). This one doesn't. Should name what someone at this level is dealing with: identity fatigue, a sense that the old self doesn't fit anymore, wanting to rebuild from a deeper place.

**Structural question:** this band shows prices before the reader knows what a class includes. It works as a teaser if the class grid section resolves it quickly enough, but the class grid is 4 sections below. The reader sees $196 and $238/mo with no anchor for what those buy. Consider adding a short format cue to each tier — "single class," "3-session pass," "deep session," "monthly" — so the price makes sense on first contact.

## 3. At a Glance

```
Eyebrow:  At a glance
H2:       One healing ecosystem, four ways in
Intro:    Every offering teaches the same Heart Center LA language: become
          aware of the pattern, release what is held, and choose the next
          committed step.
```

**"One healing ecosystem, four ways in" — strongest headline on the page.** Structural, clear, gives the reader a frame to hold. No notes.

**The intro sentence is overloaded.** It's trying to teach ARC ("become aware of the pattern, release what is held, and choose the next committed step") inside a sentence that's supposed to frame a grid of four offerings. The reader hasn't been introduced to ARC — that's two sections down. This sentence is doing the method section's job.

Simpler version: "Every offering uses the same method and the same language. The difference is the doorway." Then let the method section teach ARC.

**Card 01 — Healing Classes:**
"The primary pathway: Refresh, Reset, Restore, and Revitalize classes led through somatic, relational, and energetic practice."
- "The primary pathway" is internal hierarchy language. The reader doesn't know there's a primary vs. secondary. Drop it or replace with something the reader cares about: "The core offering" or just lead with the description.
- "Led through somatic, relational, and energetic practice" — "energetic" means nothing specific to a cold visitor. Could be CrossFit. Consider "body-based, relational, and breathwork practice."

**Card 02 — Conscious Connections:**
"Thursday night relational practice and conscious dating in Pasadena, structured around breathwork and guided connection."
- This is the best card. Day, place, format, all in one sentence. "Structured around breathwork and guided connection" — "breathwork" is fine here because it's paired with a concrete format (guided connection). No notes.

**Card 03 — Heart Center Connection:**
"A weekly advice-column podcast where listener letters are walked through the ARC framework on air."
- "Walked through the ARC framework" — the reader doesn't know what ARC is yet. This is the first time the acronym appears on the page, and the section that explains it is two scrolls away. Rewrite to be self-contained: "A weekly podcast where real listener questions get honest, pattern-level answers."

**Card 04 — Institutional Facilitation:**
"Breathwork, trauma-release, and mental wellness facilitation for hospitals, teams, cultural institutions, and communities."
- Solid. Does the B2B work.
- CTA says "Inquire" — correct for B2B, but cold. "Start a conversation" is warmer without being less professional.

## 4. ARC Method

```
Eyebrow:   The method
H2:        See the pattern. Name what is held. Change what comes next.
Large:     ARC is the Heart Center LA framework for moving from awareness
           into embodied choice.
Body:      In public language, it is simple: See, Name, Change. In class,
           students move through Aware, Release, Choose and Commit. In
           deeper practice, the same arc becomes Excavate, Interpret, Restore.
CTA link:  Begin with a class
Caption:   A prepared space helps the nervous system arrive before the
           deeper work begins.
```

**The headline is the best line on the page.** Three short imperatives, each escalating. Quotable, repeatable, fits on a T-shirt. Don't touch it.

**"Moving from awareness into embodied choice"** — "embodied" is an insider word. People who already go to sound baths know what it means. People Googling "healing classes Pasadena" don't. Consider: "…for moving from seeing the pattern to choosing differently."

**The three-register paragraph is too much for a homepage.** "In public language, it is simple: See, Name, Change. In class, students move through Aware, Release, Choose and Commit. In deeper practice, the same arc becomes Excavate, Interpret, Restore." — This is internal documentation. It tells the reader there are three vocabularies for the same thing, which is interesting to a practitioner but confusing to someone deciding whether to book a $35 class. Pick one register — the public one — and let the class pages teach the rest. Suggested replacement: "In every class, the same rhythm: notice what's happening, let the body respond, choose what comes next."

**The image caption — "A prepared space helps the nervous system arrive before the deeper work begins"** — this is beautiful. It sounds like Winnie. It does brand work and education in one sentence. Protect it.

**ARC step cards:**
- Aware → "Notice the pattern" / "Breathwork, reflection, and guided attention make the loop visible enough to work with." — Clean. "The loop" is a specific, grounded word that avoids jargon. Good.
- Release → "Let the body respond" / "Somatic and energetic practices help discharge what has been held in the nervous system." — "Somatic and energetic" is two insider words in a row. "Discharge" is clinical. Consider: "Movement, breathwork, and bodywork help release what the nervous system has been holding."
- Choose → "Commit to the next step" / "Each class closes with a real choice that can be practiced outside the room." — Perfect. "Outside the room" grounds it. No notes.

## 5. Three Harmonies

```
Eyebrow:  Why it works
H2:       The Three Harmonies
Intro:    ARC is how the practice moves. The Three Harmonies are the
          worldview beneath it: the body, the natural rhythm, and the
          human relationship.
```

**The intro sentence is well constructed.** Method vs. worldview, cleanly separated. "The body, the natural rhythm, and the human relationship" — good plain-language translation of the framework. No notes.

**Self Harmony:**
"Alignment within the body, including the jaw, gut, and pelvis. This is the somatic foundation of the work Dr. Winnie Chan Wang brings to Heart Center LA."
- Jaw/gut/pelvis is concrete and differentiating. Good.
- "This is the somatic foundation of the work Dr. Winnie Chan Wang brings to Heart Center LA" — too many prepositional phrases. Tighten: "This is the body-level foundation Dr. Winnie Chan Wang brings to every class."

**Heaven + Earth + Human Harmony:**
"Daily sacred breathwork and practice that reconnect participants with timing, conditions, and a larger field of support."
- "Timing, conditions, and a larger field of support" — unusual and evocative. Captures Winnie's cosmological framing without the 4th/5th dimension language. This is the right translation layer.
- "Participants" is clinical. This section is philosophical — "you" or "the practitioner" would be warmer.

**Harmony with Other Humans:**
"Healing in relationship, naming patterns in community, and practicing a different way of being with one another."
- This is the vaguest of the three. The other two create pictures (jaw/gut/pelvis; daily breathwork, timing). This one is all abstractions. "A different way of being with one another" — what does that look like? Ground it: "Healing in relationship — naming relational patterns, practicing honest exchange, and learning to stay present with another person."

## 6. Healing Classes Grid

```
Eyebrow:  Healing classes
H2:       Choose the depth of support your system is ready for
Intro:    Start with light renewal or enter a deeper month of practice.
          The ladder is designed to meet you where you are.
```

**"Your system" is strong.** Treats the reader as a whole organism.

**"The ladder is designed to meet you where you are"** — contradicts itself. A ladder implies climbing. "Meet you where you are" implies staying put. Pick one metaphor: "The pathway is designed to meet you where you are" or "Each level meets a different need."

**Card descriptions:**
- Refresh: "A light renewal for when you are still functional, but tired." — The comma creates a deliberate pause. Honest, specific. Good.
- Reset: "A conscious interruption of a pattern so you can clear and re-orient." — "Conscious interruption" is precise. Good.
- Restore: "Repair for something that has been depleted, damaged, or worn down." — "Something" is doing real work here — it avoids naming the thing, which lets the reader fill it in. Good.
- Revitalize: "A full month of classes for renewal at a higher level than before." — "At a higher level than before" is vague. Doesn't match the specificity of the other three. The bullets say "Identity shifts / Purpose becomes clearer / New vitality has room to emerge" — those are strong. The description should match: "A full month of classes for the kind of renewal that changes how you see yourself."

**Bullet lists are active and concrete.** "Energy lifts," "Habits are interrupted," "Capacity rebuilds" — these read like outcomes, not promises. Good register.

**All four CTAs say "Reserve."** This is fine functionally. But "Reserve" x4 misses a chance to create momentum. "Start with Refresh" / "Book Reset" / "Begin Restore" / "Start Revitalize" — or even just varying the verb — would make each card feel like its own decision rather than four copies of the same button.

## 7. Award

```
Eyebrow:  Recognition
H2:       World Cultural Harmony Award recipients
Body 1:   Dr. Winnie Chan Wang and Dr. Jay Dubois, PhD received the World
          Cultural Harmony Award on May 21, 2026...
Body 2:   The recognition reflects their work fostering intercultural
          dialogue, building inclusive communities, and advancing a
          culture of peace through healing-centered practice.
CTA:      Meet the Teachers
```

**"Recipients" is passive.** They didn't just receive an envelope. The award recognizes work. Consider: "World Cultural Harmony Award, 2026" as the headline — the date does the prestige work, "recipients" doesn't.

**Body paragraph 1 is factual. Does the job.** Date, awarding bodies, both names. No notes.

**Body paragraph 2 drops out of the brand voice.** "Fostering intercultural dialogue, building inclusive communities, and advancing a culture of peace through healing-centered practice" — this reads like a press release or grant application. Every other section on this page sounds like a human talking. This one sounds like an institution writing. Rewrite in brand register: "The award recognizes what the practice has always been about — healing that starts in the body and reaches into communities, cultures, and the spaces between people."

## 8. Teachers

```
Eyebrow:  Teachers
H2:       Clinical depth, relational intelligence, and lived practice
```

**Headline maps three nouns to three credential types.** Clean. No notes.

**Winnie's bio:**
"Winnie leads the healing classes, daily sacred breathwork, private somatic sessions, and the energetic depth of Heart Center LA."
- "The energetic depth of Heart Center LA" — this will mean something to people already in the ecosystem. For a cold visitor it's opaque. If that's intentional (gatekeeping for the converted), fine. If not, swap for something concrete: "…and the somatic depth of Heart Center LA's practice."

**Winnie's credentials:**
"MIT dual BS, NYU MS, MS Oriental Medicine"
- Too compressed. Reads like a resume line, not a bio. "MIT (dual BS), NYU (MS), Master of Science in Oriental Medicine" — takes 6 more characters and becomes scannable.

**Jay's bio:**
"Jay hosts Conscious Connections, leads the podcast, teaches relational patterns, and facilitates institutional healing experiences."
- "Teaches relational patterns" — he teaches people to *recognize* relational patterns. The current phrasing could mean he teaches workshops called "Relational Patterns." Small but worth clarifying: "teaches people to recognize relational patterns."

**Jay's credentials:**
"PhD, Anthropology focus on human interaction"
- Phrasing is unusual. "PhD in Anthropology, specializing in human interaction" reads more naturally.
- "Author of Stop Leaking Power" — should be italicized if possible (*Stop Leaking Power*). It's a book title.

          nonprofit work, and facilitation relationships across Los

**"Heart Center LA's credibility is built through..."** — self-referential. You're telling the reader to trust you by explaining your credibility construction process. The logos already do that. ## Page-Level Issues

**1. Jargon sequencing.** ARC is referenced by name in the At a Glance section (card 03, podcast description) before the reader has been taught what ARC is (two sections later). Either remove the ARC reference from the podcast card or move the method section above the glance grid.

**2. No "who is this for" copy anywhere.** The page describes what Heart Center LA *does* in great detail. It never says who it's *for*. Every section assumes the reader already self-identifies as someone who needs healing. Even one sentence — in the hero subhead or the At a Glance intro — that names the feeling ("For people carrying more than they can name") would help the cold visitor self-select.

**3. The booking band appears before the product is explained.** The tier picker with prices shows up immediately after the hero, but the actual class descriptions don't appear until the 6th section. The reader is asked to price-compare ($35 vs. $238/mo) without knowing what a class includes, how long it is, or what happens in the room.

**4. No location emphasis.** The address appears in the footer only. The hero eyebrow says "Pasadena healing classes" but then the page never returns to place. If classes are in-person, the physical space is part of the product. Consider a brief "Visit us" reference near the class grid, or at minimum repeating the address near the final CTA.

**5. No student voice.** The placeholder note in the proof section acknowledges this. Until real testimonials arrive, the page is 100% brand-voice — the brand talking about itself. Even 1–2 short quotes would break that pattern and add credibility.
